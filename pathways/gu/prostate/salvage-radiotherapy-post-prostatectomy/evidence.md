# Prostate → Early salvage radiotherapy after prostatectomy

Last reviewed: 2026-09-21
Next review: 2026-12-20
Owner: GU clinic
Purpose: Decision support for biochemical recurrence or persistent PSA after radical prostatectomy
Status: current — early salvage radiotherapy is most effective at low PSA; systemic and nodal intensification must match trial risk boundaries

## Who this applies to

| Population | Direct-evidence boundary | Not established by the pivotal data |
|---|---|---|
| Detectable or rising PSA after prostatectomy | Confirm PSA trajectory, pathology, testosterone, conventional/PSMA imaging when clinically appropriate, and absence of known metastases. | Do not treat disparate PSA entry windows as identical populations. |
| GETUG-AFU 16 population | pT2–4a, pN0/x, rising PSA 0.1–2.0 ng/mL, no clinical disease. | Node-positive, overt metastatic, or PSA-outside-window disease. |
| RTOG 9601 population | pT2 margin-positive or pT3, N0, PSA 0.2–4.0 ng/mL. | An exclusively very-low-PSA population. |

## Standard options

| Clinical context | Option | Key inclusion | Typical duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biochemical recurrence after prostatectomy | Early salvage prostate-bed radiotherapy | AUA/ASTRO/SUO recommends treatment by PSA ≤0.5 ng/mL and allows consideration below 0.2 ng/mL for high-risk patients. | Field and dose individualized. |
| High-risk features with salvage radiotherapy | Add ADT | Higher-risk features include PSA ≥0.7 ng/mL, Grade Group 4–5, PSADT ≤6 months, persistent PSA, or seminal-vesicle involvement in guideline framing. | At least 4–6 months when used; longer duration may be considered for high risk. |
| Selected nodal-risk disease | Pelvic-node plus prostate-bed radiotherapy with short-course ADT | Match the SPPORT population and discuss incremental toxicity. | SPPORT used 4–6 months ADT. |

## Landmark evidence

<!-- .cross_trial -->
| Trial | Population | Intervention | Comparator | Primary endpoint | Clinically meaningful outcomes | Generalizability-critical differences |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [GETUG-AFU 16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31629656/) | pT2–4a, pN0/x, PSA 0.1–2.0, no clinical disease | Salvage radiotherapy plus two goserelin injections | Salvage radiotherapy | Progression-free survival | 120-month PFS 64% vs 49%; HR 0.54 (0.43–0.68). | Short-course ADT study; does not define long-course ADT value. |
| [RTOG 9601](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5444881/) | pT2 margin-positive or pT3, N0, PSA 0.2–4.0 | Salvage radiotherapy plus 24 months bicalutamide | Salvage radiotherapy plus placebo | Overall survival | 12-year OS 76.3% vs 71.3%; HR 0.77 (0.59–0.99). | Higher PSA window and 24-month bicalutamide strategy; not an exclusively low-PSA trial. |
| [SPPORT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35569466/) | pT2–3, N0/x, PSA 0.1–2.0 | Pelvic-node plus prostate-bed radiotherapy plus 4–6 months ADT | Prostate-bed radiotherapy alone | Five-year freedom from progression | 87.4% vs 70.9%; intermediate arm with prostate-bed RT plus ADT was 81.3%. | Three-arm trial; the node-field benefit is not an isolated radiation-only comparison. |

## Biomarkers

| Feature | Role now | Practical boundary |
|---|---|---|
| PSA and PSA doubling time | Defines recurrence kinetics and intensification risk | Use serial measurements; do not substitute PSA kinetics for staging or a trial criterion. |
| PSMA PET | Staging and target-definition information | Negative imaging does not exclude microscopic disease; match treatment to clinical risk and evidence boundary. |
| Pathology | Risk assessment | Grade Group, seminal-vesicle involvement, margin, and nodal status inform counseling. |

## Toxicity

| Regimen and comparator | Trial-level safety outcomes | Selected named toxicities | Counseling focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salvage radiotherapy plus goserelin versus radiotherapy, [GETUG-AFU 16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31629656/) | Late grade ≥3 GU events: 7% vs 8%. | Grade ≥2 hot flushes 8% vs none; sexual disorders 8% vs 5%. | Counsel on hot flashes, sexual effects, urinary symptoms, and treatment-related fatigue. |
| Salvage radiotherapy plus bicalutamide versus radiotherapy, [RTOG 9601](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5444881/) | The retrieved source set does not provide a table-wide grade ≥3 rate. | Gynecomastia 69.7% vs 10.9%. | Discuss breast symptoms, sexual effects, urinary/bowel toxicity, and prompt review of troublesome effects. |
| SPPORT intensification, [SPPORT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35569466/) | Acute grade ≥2 AEs 18%, 36%, and 44% across the three arms. | Grade ≥3 event values were not supplied in the retrieved abstract. | Discuss added acute urinary/bowel effects with pelvic-node treatment and ADT. |
