# Prostate → Early salvage radiotherapy after prostatectomy

Last reviewed: 2026-09-22
Next review: 2026-12-21
Owner: GU clinic
Purpose: Decision support for biochemical recurrence or persistent PSA after radical prostatectomy
Status: current — early salvage radiotherapy is most effective at low PSA; systemic and nodal intensification must match trial risk boundaries

## Who this applies to

| Population | Direct-evidence boundary | Not established by the pivotal data |
|---|---|---|
| Detectable or rising PSA after prostatectomy | Confirm PSA trajectory, pathology, testosterone, conventional/PSMA imaging when clinically appropriate, and absence of known metastases[^4][^5]. | Do not treat disparate PSA entry windows as identical populations[^1][^2]. |
| GETUG-AFU 16 population | pT2–4a, pN0/x, rising PSA 0.1–2.0 ng/mL, no clinical disease[^1]. | Node-positive, overt metastatic, or PSA-outside-window disease[^1]. |
| RTOG 9601 population | pT2 margin-positive or pT3, N0, PSA 0.2–4.0 ng/mL[^2]. | An exclusively very-low-PSA population[^2]. |

## Standard options

| Clinical context | Option | Key inclusion | Typical duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biochemical recurrence after prostatectomy | Early salvage prostate-bed radiotherapy[^4] | AUA/ASTRO/SUO recommends treatment by PSA ≤0.5 ng/mL and allows consideration below 0.2 ng/mL for high-risk patients[^4]. | Field and dose individualized[^4]. |
| High-risk features with salvage radiotherapy | Add ADT[^1][^2][^4] | Higher-risk features include PSA ≥0.7 ng/mL, Grade Group 4–5, PSADT ≤6 months, persistent PSA, or seminal-vesicle involvement in guideline framing[^4]. | At least 4–6 months when used; longer duration may be considered for high risk[^1][^2][^4]. |
| Selected nodal-risk disease | Pelvic-node plus prostate-bed radiotherapy with short-course ADT[^3] | Match the SPPORT population and discuss incremental toxicity[^3]. | SPPORT used 4–6 months ADT[^3]. |

## Therapy sequencing

| Recurrence state | Treatment order | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Rising or persistent PSA after prostatectomy | Evaluate PSA kinetics, pathology, testosterone, and imaging → early salvage prostate-bed radiotherapy at low PSA[^4]. | Early salvage is not a substitute for metastatic treatment when staging establishes distant disease[^4][^5]. |
| High-risk features with salvage radiotherapy | Add ADT to prostate-bed radiotherapy[^1][^2][^4]. | Duration should match the risk and pivotal trial boundary; GETUG-AFU 16 does not establish long-course ADT for every patient[^1]. |
| Selected nodal-risk disease | Prostate-bed plus pelvic-node radiotherapy with 4–6 months ADT[^3]. | SPPORT is a three-arm trial; it does not isolate a radiation-field effect without ADT[^3]. |
| PET-detected distant disease or progression after salvage | Move to a metastatic systemic-therapy pathway[^5]. | This page does not establish a preferred systemic sequence after salvage radiotherapy[^5]. |

## Surveillance and treatment de-escalation

| Phase | Monitoring cadence | Stop, hold, or de-escalate | Re-escalation / next line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before and during salvage RT[^4] | PSA, testosterone, and imaging to exclude distant disease; treat at PSA ≤0.5 ng/mL when feasible (≤0.2 ng/mL for high-risk features per society guidance)[^4]. | Complete radiotherapy course; ADT duration matches trial (GETUG-AFU 16: two goserelin injections; RTOG 9601: 24 months bicalutamide; SPPORT: 4–6 months ADT with nodal fields)[^1][^2][^3]. | Progression after salvage → ADT intensification, metastatic systemic therapy, or repeat local assessment[^4][^5]. |
| After salvage RT ± ADT[^4] | PSA every 3–6 months; monitor ADT side effects and bone health[^4][^6]. | Stop short-course ADT at planned end; long-course bicalutamide completes 24 months unless toxicity[^1][^2][^3]. | Rising PSA with imaging-confirmed metastases → mHSPC/mCRPC pathways[^5]. |

- Pelvic-node radiotherapy plus short ADT (SPPORT) is for selected nodal-risk disease; do not extend nodal fields without trial-consistent indications[^3].

## Landmark evidence

| Trial | Population | Intervention | Comparator | Primary endpoint | Clinically meaningful outcomes | Generalizability-critical differences |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [GETUG-AFU 16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31629656/) | pT2–4a, pN0/x, PSA 0.1–2.0, no clinical disease[^1] | Salvage radiotherapy plus two goserelin injections[^1] | Salvage radiotherapy[^1] | Progression-free survival[^1] | 120-month PFS 64% vs 49%; HR 0.54 (0.43–0.68)[^1]. | Short-course ADT study; does not define long-course ADT value[^1]. |
| [RTOG 9601](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5444881/) | pT2 margin-positive or pT3, N0, PSA 0.2–4.0[^2] | Salvage radiotherapy plus 24 months bicalutamide[^2] | Salvage radiotherapy plus placebo[^2] | Overall survival[^2] | 12-year OS 76.3% vs 71.3%; HR 0.77 (0.59–0.99)[^2]. | Higher PSA window and 24-month bicalutamide strategy; not an exclusively low-PSA trial[^2]. |
| [SPPORT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35569466/) | pT2–3, N0/x, PSA 0.1–2.0[^3] | Pelvic-node plus prostate-bed radiotherapy plus 4–6 months ADT[^3] | Prostate-bed radiotherapy alone[^3] | Five-year freedom from progression[^3] | 87.4% vs 70.9%; intermediate arm with prostate-bed RT plus ADT was 81.3%[^3]. | Three-arm trial; the node-field benefit is not an isolated radiation-only comparison[^3]. |

## Biomarkers

| Feature | Role now | Practical boundary |
|---|---|---|
| PSA and PSA doubling time | Defines recurrence kinetics and intensification risk[^4] | Use serial measurements; do not substitute PSA kinetics for staging or a trial criterion[^1][^2][^4]. |
| PSMA PET | Staging and target-definition information[^4][^5] | Negative imaging does not exclude microscopic disease; match treatment to clinical risk and evidence boundary[^4]. |
| Pathology | Risk assessment[^4] | Grade Group, seminal-vesicle involvement, margin, and nodal status inform counseling[^4]. |

## Toxicity

| Regimen and comparator | Trial-level safety outcomes | Selected named toxicities | Counseling focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salvage radiotherapy plus goserelin versus radiotherapy, [GETUG-AFU 16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31629656/) | Late grade ≥3 GU events: 7% vs 8%[^1]. | Grade ≥2 hot flushes 8% vs none; sexual disorders 8% vs 5%[^1]. | Counsel on hot flashes, sexual effects, urinary symptoms, and treatment-related fatigue[^1]. |
| Salvage radiotherapy plus bicalutamide versus radiotherapy, [RTOG 9601](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5444881/) | The retrieved source set does not provide a table-wide grade ≥3 rate[^2]. | Gynecomastia 69.7% vs 10.9%[^2]. | Discuss breast symptoms, sexual effects, urinary/bowel toxicity, and prompt review of troublesome effects[^2]. |
| SPPORT intensification, [SPPORT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35569466/) | Acute grade ≥2 AEs 18%, 36%, and 44% across the three arms[^3]. | Grade ≥3 event values were not supplied in the retrieved abstract[^3]. | Discuss added acute urinary/bowel effects with pelvic-node treatment and ADT[^3]. |

### On-treatment monitoring

| SOC/option regimen | Hallmark monitoring toxicity and published rate | Patient action / clinic response |
|---|---|---|
| Salvage radiotherapy plus goserelin | GETUG-AFU 16: late grade ≥3 GU events 7% vs 8%; grade ≥2 hot flushes 8% vs none[^1]. | Monitor urinary/bowel symptoms, hot flashes, sexual function, fatigue, metabolic and bone effects; urinary retention, heavy bleeding, or severe bowel symptoms needs prompt contact[^1]. |
| Salvage radiotherapy plus bicalutamide | RTOG 9601: gynecomastia 69.7% vs 10.9%[^2]. | Review breast symptoms, sexual effects, urinary/bowel symptoms, and fatigue; troublesome breast pain or swelling, retention, heavy bleeding, or severe bowel symptoms needs prompt contact[^2]. |
| Prostate-bed plus pelvic-node radiotherapy with short-course ADT | SPPORT: acute grade ≥2 AEs 18%, 36%, and 44% across the three arms; the retrieved abstract did not report a comparable grade ≥3 named rate[^3]. | Monitor added acute urinary/bowel symptoms and ADT effects; severe diarrhea, retention, heavy bleeding, or dehydration needs prompt contact[^3]. |
