# Prostate → Adjuvant radiotherapy after prostatectomy

Last reviewed: 2026-09-22
Next review: 2026-12-21
Owner: GU clinic
Purpose: Decision support for adverse-pathology prostate cancer with an undetectable postoperative PSA
Status: current — modern trials support surveillance with early salvage radiotherapy for the studied population rather than routine adjuvant radiotherapy

## Who this applies to

| Population | Trial criteria or boundary | Decision implication |
|---|---|---|
| Adverse pathology after radical prostatectomy with PSA ≤0.2 ng/mL | RADICALS-RT, RAVES, and GETUG-AFU 17 randomized within roughly 22 weeks after surgery[^1][^2][^3]. | Surveillance and protocol-defined early salvage are the direct modern-trial comparison[^4]. |
| Persistent PSA, pathologic node-positive disease, or known metastatic disease | Outside the modern ART-versus-early-salvage population[^4]. | Use a setting-specific salvage/intensification discussion; do not import this result unchanged[^4][^6]. |

## Standard options

| Clinical context | Option | Key inclusion | Typical course |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adverse pathology with undetectable PSA | PSA surveillance with early salvage radiotherapy if indicated[^4] | Modern ARTISTIC-type population[^4]. | Trigger and field individualized to PSA kinetics, pathology, and imaging[^4][^6]. |
| Selected exceptional postoperative risk | Adjuvant radiotherapy after multidisciplinary discussion[^5][^6] | Do not treat the randomized evidence as a blanket rule for persistent PSA or pN+ disease[^4]. | Individualized[^5][^6]. |

## Therapy sequencing

| Postoperative state | Treatment order | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Adverse pathology with undetectable PSA | PSA surveillance → early salvage radiotherapy if recurrence criteria are met[^4]. | This is the modern randomized comparison and does not apply unchanged to persistent PSA or pN+ disease[^4]. |
| Persistent PSA, nodal disease, or imaging-detected recurrence | Enter the early-salvage/intensification pathway rather than routine-adjuvant evidence[^6]. | These states were outside the modern ART-versus-early-salvage trial population[^4]. |
| Exceptional postoperative risk after multidisciplinary review | Consider adjuvant radiotherapy[^5][^6]. | ART is not a blanket sequence; balance recurrence risk against durable urinary and sexual toxicity[^1][^2][^3]. |

## Surveillance and treatment de-escalation

| Phase | Monitoring cadence | Stop, hold, or de-escalate | Re-escalation / next line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Post-prostatectomy surveillance (preferred)[^4][^6] | PSA every 3–6 months within the first 2 years, then every 6 months through 5 years; testosterone if ADT ever used[^5][^6][^7]. | Do not deliver routine adjuvant radiotherapy when PSA is undetectable in the ARTISTIC population; observation with protocol-defined early salvage is the modern comparison[^4]. | PSA rise above institutional early-salvage trigger → salvage radiotherapy pathway while PSA remains low[^4][^6]. |
| Selected adjuvant radiotherapy[^1][^5] | PSA every 3–6 months; urinary and sexual toxicity assessment[^1][^5]. | Complete planned radiotherapy course; hold only for acute toxicity[^1]. | Biochemical or imaging progression → salvage intensification or systemic therapy[^5][^6]. |

- AUA/ASTRO/SUO support early salvage when PSA rises rather than automatic adjuvant radiotherapy for the studied adverse-pathology, undetectable-PSA population[^6].

## Landmark evidence

| Trial | Population | Intervention | Comparator | Primary endpoint | Clinically meaningful outcomes | Generalizability-critical differences |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [RADICALS-RT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38583574/), N=1,396 | ≥1 adverse feature after prostatectomy[^1] | Adjuvant prostate-bed radiotherapy[^1] | Observation with salvage-policy radiotherapy[^1] | Freedom from distant metastasis[^1] | Ten-year FFDM 93% vs 90%; HR 0.68 (0.43–1.07); OS HR 0.98[^1]. | Includes varied adverse features; not a persistent-PSA strategy[^1]. |
| [RAVES](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33002437/), N=333 | Margin positivity, extraprostatic extension, or seminal-vesicle invasion; PSA ≤0.10[^2] | Adjuvant radiotherapy[^2] | Early salvage radiotherapy[^2] | Five-year freedom from biochemical progression[^2] | 86% vs 87%; salvage/adjuvant HR 1.12 (0.65–1.90)[^2]. | Did not meet prespecified noninferiority because events were fewer than expected[^2]. |
| [GETUG-AFU 17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33002438/) | pT3–4a/R1, pN0/x; all received 6 months triptorelin[^3] | Adjuvant radiotherapy plus triptorelin[^3] | Early salvage radiotherapy plus triptorelin[^3] | Event-free survival[^3] | Five-year EFS 92% vs 90%; HR 0.81 (0.48–1.36)[^3]. | Hormone therapy in both arms; not a radiation-alone comparison[^3]. |
| [ARTISTIC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7611137/), N=2,153 | Meta-analysis of the three modern trials[^4] | Adjuvant-radiotherapy policy[^4] | Early-salvage policy[^4] | Event-free survival[^4] | Five-year EFS 89% vs 88%; HR 0.95 (0.75–1.21)[^4]. | Only 39% in the early-salvage-policy group had started radiotherapy at analysis[^4]. |

## Biomarkers

| Feature | Role now | Practical boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Postoperative PSA | Defines the undetectable-PSA trial boundary[^1][^2][^4] | Persistent PSA requires a salvage rather than routine-adjuvant discussion[^4][^6]. |
| Pathology | Recurrence-risk assessment[^1][^2][^3] | Margin, grade, extracapsular extension, and seminal-vesicle invasion characterize risk but do not mandate routine ART in the modern-trial population[^4]. |

## Toxicity

| Regimen and comparator | Trial-level safety outcomes | Selected named toxicities | Counseling focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adjuvant versus early-salvage radiotherapy, [RADICALS-RT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38583574/) | Grade 3–4 urethral stricture within 2 years: 6% vs 4%[^1]. | One-year urinary incontinence 5.3% vs 2.7%[^1]. | Review urinary leakage, obstruction, hematuria, bowel change, and sexual dysfunction; contact the team for retention or heavy bleeding[^1]. |
| Adjuvant versus early-salvage radiotherapy, [RAVES](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33002437/) | Grade ≥2 GU toxicity: 70% vs 54%[^2]. | The retrieved abstract does not provide named-event percentages[^2]. | Counsel on urinary and sexual effects and avoid adding unverified event-level rates[^2]. |
| Adjuvant versus early-salvage radiotherapy, [GETUG-AFU 17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33002438/) | Late grade ≥2 GU toxicity: 59% vs 22%[^3]. | Late grade ≥2 erectile dysfunction: 28% vs 8%[^3]. | Discuss durable urinary and erectile effects before choosing treatment timing[^3]. |

### On-treatment monitoring

| SOC/option regimen | Hallmark monitoring toxicity and published rate | Patient action / clinic response |
|---|---|---|
| Adjuvant or early-salvage prostate-bed radiotherapy | RADICALS-RT: grade 3–4 urethral stricture within two years 6% vs 4%; one-year urinary incontinence 5.3% vs 2.7%[^1]. | Monitor urinary leakage, obstruction, retention, hematuria, bowel change, and sexual function; retention or heavy bleeding requires prompt contact[^1]. |
| Radiotherapy plus six months of triptorelin in GETUG-AFU 17 | Late grade ≥2 GU toxicity 59% vs 22%; late grade ≥2 erectile dysfunction 28% vs 8%[^3]. | Review urinary, bowel, erectile, hot-flash, fatigue, metabolic, and bone symptoms; retention, heavy bleeding, or severe bowel symptoms needs prompt contact[^3]. |
