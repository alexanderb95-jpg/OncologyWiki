# Prostate → Adjuvant radiotherapy after prostatectomy

Last reviewed: 2026-09-21
Next review: 2026-12-20
Owner: GU clinic
Purpose: Decision support for adverse-pathology prostate cancer with an undetectable postoperative PSA
Status: current — modern trials support surveillance with early salvage radiotherapy for the studied population rather than routine adjuvant radiotherapy

## Who this applies to

| Population | Trial criteria or boundary | Decision implication |
|---|---|---|
| Adverse pathology after radical prostatectomy with PSA ≤0.2 ng/mL | RADICALS-RT, RAVES, and GETUG-AFU 17 randomized within roughly 22 weeks after surgery. | Surveillance and protocol-defined early salvage are the direct modern-trial comparison. |
| Persistent PSA, pathologic node-positive disease, or known metastatic disease | Outside the modern ART-versus-early-salvage population. | Use a setting-specific salvage/intensification discussion; do not import this result unchanged. |

## Standard options

| Clinical context | Option | Key inclusion | Typical course |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adverse pathology with undetectable PSA | PSA surveillance with early salvage radiotherapy if indicated | Modern ARTISTIC-type population. | Trigger and field individualized to PSA kinetics, pathology, and imaging. |
| Selected exceptional postoperative risk | Adjuvant radiotherapy after multidisciplinary discussion | Do not treat the randomized evidence as a blanket rule for persistent PSA or pN+ disease. | Individualized. |

## Landmark evidence

<!-- .cross_trial -->
| Trial | Population | Intervention | Comparator | Primary endpoint | Clinically meaningful outcomes | Generalizability-critical differences |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [RADICALS-RT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38583574/), N=1,396 | ≥1 adverse feature after prostatectomy | Adjuvant prostate-bed radiotherapy | Observation with salvage-policy radiotherapy | Freedom from distant metastasis | Ten-year FFDM 93% vs 90%; HR 0.68 (0.43–1.07); OS HR 0.98. | Includes varied adverse features; not a persistent-PSA strategy. |
| [RAVES](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33002437/), N=333 | Margin positivity, extraprostatic extension, or seminal-vesicle invasion; PSA ≤0.10 | Adjuvant radiotherapy | Early salvage radiotherapy | Five-year freedom from biochemical progression | 86% vs 87%; salvage/adjuvant HR 1.12 (0.65–1.90). | Did not meet prespecified noninferiority because events were fewer than expected. |
| [GETUG-AFU 17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33002438/) | pT3–4a/R1, pN0/x; all received 6 months triptorelin | Adjuvant radiotherapy plus triptorelin | Early salvage radiotherapy plus triptorelin | Event-free survival | Five-year EFS 92% vs 90%; HR 0.81 (0.48–1.36). | Hormone therapy in both arms; not a radiation-alone comparison. |
| [ARTISTIC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7611137/), N=2,153 | Meta-analysis of the three modern trials | Adjuvant-radiotherapy policy | Early-salvage policy | Event-free survival | Five-year EFS 89% vs 88%; HR 0.95 (0.75–1.21). | Only 39% in the early-salvage-policy group had started radiotherapy at analysis. |

## Biomarkers

| Feature | Role now | Practical boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Postoperative PSA | Defines the undetectable-PSA trial boundary | Persistent PSA requires a salvage rather than routine-adjuvant discussion. |
| Pathology | Recurrence-risk assessment | Margin, grade, extracapsular extension, and seminal-vesicle invasion characterize risk but do not mandate routine ART in the modern-trial population. |

## Toxicity

| Regimen and comparator | Trial-level safety outcomes | Selected named toxicities | Counseling focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adjuvant versus early-salvage radiotherapy, [RADICALS-RT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38583574/) | Grade 3–4 urethral stricture within 2 years: 6% vs 4%. | One-year urinary incontinence 5.3% vs 2.7%. | Review urinary leakage, obstruction, hematuria, bowel change, and sexual dysfunction; contact the team for retention or heavy bleeding. |
| Adjuvant versus early-salvage radiotherapy, [RAVES](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33002437/) | Grade ≥2 GU toxicity: 70% vs 54%. | The retrieved abstract does not provide named-event percentages. | Counsel on urinary and sexual effects and avoid adding unverified event-level rates. |
| Adjuvant versus early-salvage radiotherapy, [GETUG-AFU 17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33002438/) | Late grade ≥2 GU toxicity: 59% vs 22%. | Late grade ≥2 erectile dysfunction: 28% vs 8%. | Discuss durable urinary and erectile effects before choosing treatment timing. |
