- Counseling: Modern trials (ARTISTIC meta-analysis) showed similar 5-year event-free survival with adjuvant vs early-salvage radiotherapy policy (about 89% vs 88%; HR 0.95 vs early salvage). RADICALS-RT 10-year freedom from distant metastasis 93% vs 90% with early-salvage policy (HR 0.68 vs early salvage, CI crossed 1). Observation with early salvage at low PSA is preferred for undetectable PSA after adverse pathology.
- Counseling: Reviewed surveillance and de-escalation: PSA every 3–6 months within the first 2 years, then every 6 months through 5 years when observing after adverse pathology with undetectable PSA; early salvage RT at low PSA rise rather than routine adjuvant RT for the modern-trial population (ARTISTIC published 5-year EFS about 89% vs 88% for adjuvant vs early-salvage policy; not a full KM curve); persistent PSA or pN+ needs separate intensification discussion.
- Monitoring: During radiotherapy, review urinary leakage/obstruction, retention, hematuria, bowel change, and sexual function; if triptorelin is used, also review hot flashes, fatigue, metabolic, and bone effects. Contact the team for urinary retention, heavy hematuria, or new severe bowel symptoms; late urinary and erectile effects can persist.
