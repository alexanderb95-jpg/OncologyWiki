# Prostate → nmCRPC

Last reviewed: 2026-09-23
Next review: 2026-12-22
Owner: GU clinic
Purpose: Shared decision support for high-risk nonmetastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer
Status: current — three placebo-controlled phase 3 trials support androgen-receptor pathway inhibition with continued ADT in the conventional-imaging, high-risk population

## Who this applies to

### Pivotal-trial eligibility and decision boundary

| Trial | Eligible population | Key imaging and clinical boundary | Outside direct pivotal evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| [PROSPER](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8288034/) | Castration-resistant adenocarcinoma, PSADT ≤10 months, continued ADT[^1] | CT/MRI and radionuclide bone scan without metastases; seizure history or predisposition was excluded[^1]. | PSADT >10 months, prior or current conventional-imaging metastases, or seizure-risk population[^1]. |
| [SPARTAN](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1715546) | PSADT ≤10 months, continued ADT[^3] | Technetium bone scan and CT without distant metastases; pelvic N1 nodes <2 cm below the aortic bifurcation were allowed[^3]. | Conventional-imaging distant metastases or disease outside the trial definition[^3]. |
| [ARAMIS](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1815671) | PSADT ≤10 months, PSA ≥2 ng/mL, ECOG 0–1, continued ADT[^5] | CT/MRI and radionuclide bone scan without detectable or prior metastases; pelvic nodes <2 cm below the aortic bifurcation were allowed[^5]. | ECOG status outside the study population, conventional-imaging metastases, or disease outside the trial definition[^5]. |

- PSMA-PET-only disease, PSA doubling time >10 months, and failure to meet the castration definition lack direct pivotal-trial efficacy estimates[^1][^3][^5][^7].
- Confirm castrate testosterone, PSA kinetics, conventional imaging, prior androgen-receptor pathway inhibitor exposure, falls/fracture risk, blood pressure, cognition, cardiovascular comorbidity, and patient priorities before selecting treatment[^10][^11].

## Standard options

| Clinical group | Option | Evidence boundary | Typical course |
|---|---|---|---|
| High-risk nmCRPC within a pivotal-trial population | Enzalutamide plus continued ADT[^1] | PROSPER used conventional-imaging nonmetastatic disease and PSADT ≤10 months[^1]. | Enzalutamide 160 mg orally daily with continued ADT; duration in the source pack is not stated[^1]. |
| High-risk nmCRPC within a pivotal-trial population | Apalutamide plus continued ADT[^3] | SPARTAN used conventional-imaging nonmetastatic disease and PSADT ≤10 months[^3]. | Apalutamide 240 mg orally daily with continued ADT; duration in the source pack is not stated[^3]. |
| High-risk nmCRPC within a pivotal-trial population | Darolutamide plus continued ADT[^5] | ARAMIS used conventional-imaging nonmetastatic disease and PSADT ≤10 months[^5]. | Darolutamide 600 mg orally twice daily with food and continued ADT; duration in the source pack is not stated[^5]. |
| Outside the direct pivotal population or unable to use an ARPI | Individualized surveillance, supportive management, or clinical-trial evaluation[^10] | Do not extrapolate the three phase 3 efficacy estimates to this group[^1][^3][^5]. | Individualized[^10]. |

The pivotal trials do not provide a head-to-head comparison or ranking of apalutamide, darolutamide, and enzalutamide[^1][^3][^5].

## Therapy sequencing

| Disease state | Treatment order | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| High-risk nmCRPC on conventional imaging | Continue ADT → add one of enzalutamide, apalutamide, or darolutamide[^1][^3][^5]. | The pivotal trials do not compare the agents head-to-head and require PSADT ≤10 months with conventional-imaging M0 disease[^1][^3][^5]. |
| PSMA-PET-only disease or PSADT >10 months | Individualize surveillance, staging, or trial evaluation[^7][^10]. | Direct phase 3 treatment-selection evidence is absent for these states[^1][^3][^5]. |
| Conventional-imaging metastasis or progression while castrate | Continue ADT and transition to the mCRPC pathway with prior ARPI exposure documented[^10]. | Do not presume sequential ARPI benefit from these trials[^1][^3][^5]. |

## Surveillance and treatment de-escalation

| Setting | PSA | Imaging | Stop/de-escalate | Re-escalate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| On ADT plus ARPI[^1][^3][^5] | Every 3 months; testosterone to confirm castration; track PSADT for risk context[^10][^11]. | Conventional CT and bone scan every 6–12 months per EAU/NCCN risk-adapted frameworks[^10][^11]. | Continue ADT plus the selected ARPI until conventional-imaging metastasis, unacceptable toxicity, or shared decision to stop. Pivotal trials did not define a fixed treatment-stop duration. Hold ARPI for grade ≥3 toxicity; do not stop ADT at M0 progression on PET alone outside trial criteria[^1][^3][^5][^10]. | Conventional-imaging metastasis while castrate → mCRPC pathway; document prior ARPI agent and duration[^10]. |
| Surveillance-only (outside pivotal population)[^10] | Every 3 months; continue PSADT assessment[^10][^11]. | Imaging per PSADT and clinical concern[^10][^11]. | No ARPI if PSADT >10 months or PSMA-PET-only disease without conventional metastasis—individualize[^1][^7][^10]. | Rising PSA with new conventional metastases → mCRPC sequencing[^10]. |

- PSMA-PET-only disease lacks direct pivotal-trial efficacy estimates; do not use PET alone to stop ADT or ARPI without conventional-imaging confirmation when clinically appropriate[^7][^10].

## Landmark evidence

The phase 3 studies were placebo-controlled, conventional-imaging nmCRPC trials. The final OS time points differ—median OS in PROSPER and SPARTAN versus 3-year OS in ARAMIS—and must not be compared across trials[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6].

## Biomarkers

| Marker | Current clinic role | Emerging / investigational | How it changes decision now | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PSA doubling time | Trial risk-enrichment and eligibility factor[^1][^3][^5] | — | A PSADT ≤10 months defined the pivotal-trial population for ARPI with continued ADT[^1][^3][^5]. | Not an agent-selecting biomarker among enzalutamide, apalutamide, and darolutamide[^1][^3][^5]. |
| Conventional imaging M0 definition | Disease-state classification boundary[^1][^3][^5] | — | Phase 3 evidence used conventional CT/MRI and bone imaging to define nonmetastatic disease eligible for treatment[^1][^3][^5]. | Pelvic N1 nodes <2 cm were allowed in SPARTAN/ARAMIS; distant metastases exclude this setting[^3][^5]. |
| PSMA PET | Staging information[^7] | Prospective treatment-guidance studies (e.g., PSMACare)[^8] | May find occult M1 disease despite negative conventional imaging; does not by itself select among the three ARPIs[^7][^8]. | In one retrospective 200-patient high-risk cohort, PSMA PET found M1 disease in 55% despite negative conventional imaging; prospective treatment-guidance value remains unestablished[^7]. |
| Molecular testing | No validated treatment selector among the three ARPIs[^1][^3][^5] | Broader panels for later-line planning after progression[^10] | Does not choose enzalutamide vs apalutamide vs darolutamide on this page[^1][^3][^5]. | Pivotal eligibility texts did not report molecular enrollment criteria that select among the three agents[^1][^3][^5]. |

## Gene and cellular therapy

No FDA-approved CAR-T, TCR-T, TIL, or gene-edited cell product is standard for nonmetastatic CRPC on this page[^10]. ARPI options on this page are not gene or cellular therapy[^1][^2][^3]. Investigational cellular therapy is concentrated in metastatic CRPC, not nmCRPC SOC[^14][^15].

| Role | Content |
|---|---|
| Current / approved clinic role | None for nmCRPC[^10]. |
| Emerging / investigational | No verified nmCRPC gene/cellular practice-changing program; discuss mCRPC cellular-therapy trials if/when metastatic progression occurs[^14][^15]. |

## Upcoming research

Field direction: PSMA-directed approaches in conventional-imaging M0 / PET-positive CRPC and real-world ARPI delivery data may refine nmCRPC counseling, but do not yet replace the three ARPI phase 3 standards[^8][^9][^10].

No new practice-changing randomized result was identified in the source pack[^10].

| Trial or publication | Why it may change care | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| [PSMACare](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05849298), NCT05849298 | Tests lutetium-177 vipivotide tetraxetan alone or with an androgen-receptor pathway inhibitor in PSMA-PET-positive, conventional-imaging-M0 CRPC; the registry permits PET-only M1 disease[^8]. | Active, not recruiting; randomized noncomparative phase 2; actual N=49; no posted outcomes[^8]. |
| [DAROL](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41176520/) | Observational real-world darolutamide experience may inform delivery, but does not compare agents or establish a new treatment standard[^9]. | Peer-reviewed observational interim report[^9]. |

## Toxicity

| Regimen and comparator | Trial-level safety outcomes | Selected named toxicities | Counseling focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enzalutamide + ADT versus placebo + ADT, [PROSPER](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8288034/) | Grade ≥3 AEs: 31% vs 23%; discontinuation due to AEs: 9% vs 6%[^1]. | Fatigue 33% vs 14%; hypertension 12% vs 5%; falls 11% vs 4%; dizziness 10% vs 4%; falls/nonpathologic fractures combined 17% vs 8%[^1]. | Contact oncology for marked fatigue, new dizziness, a fall, possible fracture, or blood-pressure concerns; interruption or discontinuation may be needed[^1]. |
| Apalutamide + ADT versus placebo + ADT, [SPARTAN](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29420164/) | Grade 3–4 AEs: 45.1% vs 34.2%; discontinuation due to AEs: 10.6% vs 7.0%[^3]. | Fatigue 30.4% vs 21.1%; hypertension 24.8% vs 19.8%; rash 23.8% vs 5.5%; falls 15.6% vs 9.0%; fracture 11.7% vs 6.5%; hypothyroidism 8.1% vs 2.0%[^3]. | Contact oncology for extensive rash, marked fatigue, a fall or suspected fracture, or blood-pressure concerns; treatment interruption or discontinuation may be needed[^3]. |
| Darolutamide + ADT versus placebo + ADT, [ARAMIS](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30763142/) | Grade 3–4 AEs: 24.7% vs 19.5%; discontinuation due to AEs: 8.9% vs 8.7%[^5]. | Fatigue 12.1% vs 8.7%; hypertension 6.6% vs 5.2%[^5]. | Contact oncology for worsening fatigue, palpitations, chest symptoms, or blood-pressure concerns; treatment interruption or discontinuation may be needed[^5]. |

### Patient counseling and action plan

- Contact oncology promptly for a new extensive rash, marked fatigue, a fall or suspected fracture, marked dizziness, new confusion or memory change, palpitations, chest symptoms, or another concerning treatment effect. *** after-hours/urgent-assessment instructions reviewed[^1][^3][^5].
- Maintain fall and fracture-risk assessment, blood-pressure monitoring, and thyroid assessment where clinically indicated; treatment holds or discontinuation may be required[^1][^3][^5].
