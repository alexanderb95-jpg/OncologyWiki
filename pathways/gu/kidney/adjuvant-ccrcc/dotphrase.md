- Counseling: Reviewed pathology-based recurrence risk after nephrectomy with the KEYNOTE-564 eligibility calculator: {{calc:keynote-564|KEYNOTE-564 eligibility}}. This calculator classifies the published trial-risk category; it does not replace pathology review or patient-specific treatment assessment.
- Counseling: Reviewed adjuvant pembrolizumab and risk-adapted surveillance. In KEYNOTE-564, pembrolizumab was compared with placebo after surgery, not with surveillance. At 5 years, estimated disease-free survival was 60.9% with pembrolizumab versus 52.2% with placebo, and overall survival was 87.7% versus 82.3%. These results apply to the trial population and do not establish benefit in lower-risk or non-clear-cell RCC.
- Counseling: For M1 NED, reviewed that the trial required complete resection of the primary tumor and a solid, isolated soft-tissue metastasis at nephrectomy or within 1 year. The subgroup was small; multidisciplinary review is needed rather than a standalone efficacy estimate.
- Counseling: Reviewed pembrolizumab toxicity in KEYNOTE-564: grade ≥3 all-cause AEs were 32%, 21% discontinued because of an AE, immune-mediated AEs occurred in 36%, and high-dose corticosteroids were used in 8%. About 21 in 100 developed hypothyroidism and 13 in 100 hyperthyroidism; grade 3 adrenal insufficiency, diabetic ketoacidosis, and type 1 diabetes each occurred in about 1 in 100. Reviewed prompt contact for fatigue, heat/cold intolerance, palpitations, dizziness, severe headache, thirst, confusion, or nausea. Hypothyroidism can require long-term replacement.
- Counseling: Reviewed bowel and liver toxicity with pembrolizumab: grade 3 diarrhea occurred in 2 in 100, colitis in 1 in 100, ALT elevation in 2 in 100, and AST elevation in 1 in 100; grade 3/4 hepatitis, liver injury, cholestasis, and pancreatic-enzyme abnormalities were each <1%. Instructed to contact oncology the same day for diarrhea, severe abdominal pain, fever, blood/mucus in stool, jaundice, dark urine, severe nausea/vomiting, or unusual bruising. *** after-hours/urgent-assessment instructions reviewed.
- Counseling: Reviewed lung, kidney, and skin toxicity with pembrolizumab: pneumonitis occurred in 1.6%; grade 3/4 pneumonitis, grade 3 nephritis/acute kidney injury, and grade 3 Stevens–Johnson syndrome were each <1%. Instructed to call promptly for new/worsening cough, chest pain, shortness of breath, markedly reduced urine, or new swelling; marked breathing difficulty or a widespread painful, blistering, peeling, or mucosal rash needs urgent assessment.
- Counseling: Reviewed the remaining rare grade 3/4 treatment-related toxicities in KEYNOTE-564: grade 3 hypertension was 1%; cardiovascular, neurologic, hematologic, and other events were otherwise each <1%. Instructed to seek urgent assessment for chest pain, fainting, palpitations, focal neurologic symptoms, severe weakness, abnormal bleeding, or severe infection symptoms. Immune effects can occur after therapy and may require holding treatment, corticosteroids, hormone replacement, or specialist care.
- Counseling: If considering pembrolizumab plus belzutifan, reviewed that it is FDA-labeled in the US for eligible clear-cell RCC. LITESPARK-022 showed a DFS benefit versus pembrolizumab plus placebo; overall-survival data were immature. Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent AEs were 52.1% versus 30.2%. Anemia occurred in 84% (grade ≥3, 12.1%) and hypoxia in 7% (grade ≥3, 4.6%); instructed to call promptly for worsening fatigue, dizziness, palpitations, chest discomfort, dyspnea, or a low home pulse-oximeter reading. It is not presented as proven to improve overall survival.
- Counseling: For pembrolizumab plus belzutifan, reviewed grade ≥3 ALT elevation (6.4%), plus the same-trial label-table grade 3–4 rates for fatigue (3.1%), diarrhea (3.0%), laboratory ALT (13%), AST (8%), and decreased lymphocytes (9%). Instructed to keep CBC/liver testing and report jaundice, dark urine, persistent diarrhea, severe nausea, disabling fatigue/dizziness, fever, or infection symptoms promptly.

- Labs/NGS: Documented KEYNOTE-564 risk assessment: {{calc:keynote-564|KEYNOTE-564 eligibility}}; confirmed histology *** and risk features ***. Obtain CBC, CMP with creatinine and liver tests, and thyroid testing per institutional protocol. For belzutifan, monitor hemoglobin and oxygen saturation before treatment and periodically; severe anemia or hypoxia may require transfusion, oxygen, and a dose hold/reduction/discontinuation. Review pregnancy-prevention counseling per label. Tumor NGS and ctDNA are not required to select adjuvant therapy.
- Procedures: *** postoperative restaging and multidisciplinary review completed / planned. For non-clear-cell histology or disease outside KEYNOTE-564 criteria, favor surveillance or clinical-trial review rather than extrapolating pembrolizumab benefit.
- Medications: If selected, pembrolizumab monotherapy is 200 mg IV every 3 weeks for up to 17 cycles. Pembrolizumab plus belzutifan is pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks or 400 mg IV every 6 weeks plus belzutifan 120 mg orally daily, for up to 1 year; confirm current FDA label and local formulary.

- Follow-up: *** risk-adapted chest and abdominal surveillance; review new immune-related symptoms and renal function at each visit.
