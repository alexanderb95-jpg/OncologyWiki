# Testis → Metastatic germ-cell tumor

Last reviewed: 2026-09-22
Next review: 2026-12-21
Owner: GU clinic
Purpose: Decision support for stage IIC/III or systemic germ-cell tumor requiring first-line chemotherapy
Status: current — treatment is IGCCCG-directed and should be coordinated by an experienced germ-cell tumor team

## Who this applies to

| Population | Definition | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Metastatic seminoma or NSGCT | Use prechemotherapy AFP, hCG, LDH, primary site, and nonpulmonary visceral metastases for IGCCCG classification[^1][^2]. | Do not use pre-orchiectomy markers alone[^1][^5]. |
| Good-risk NSGCT | Testis/retroperitoneal primary, no nonpulmonary visceral metastases, and AFP/hCG/LDH below IGCCCG intermediate thresholds[^1]. | Stage IIA/B marker-negative low-volume disease follows a separate RPLND/radiotherapy/chemotherapy decision pathway[^5]. |
| Intermediate-/poor-risk NSGCT or intermediate-risk seminoma | Match exact IGCCCG group and organ burden[^1][^2]. | No poor-prognosis IGCCCG seminoma category exists[^2]. |

## Standard options

| Clinical context | Option | Key inclusion | Typical course |
|---|---|---|---|
| Good-risk seminoma or NSGCT | BEP ×3[^3] | Cisplatin-based first-line treatment[^3][^5]. | Three cycles[^3]. |
| Good-risk disease with bleomycin contraindication | EP ×4[^5] | Bleomycin cannot be used[^5]. | Four cycles[^5]. |
| Intermediate-risk seminoma or intermediate-/poor-risk NSGCT | BEP ×4[^1][^5] | IGCCCG risk group[^1]. | Four cycles[^5]. |
| Bleomycin contraindication in intermediate-/poor-risk disease | VIP ×4 with primary G-CSF[^5] | Expert-center management[^5]. | Four cycles[^5]. |
| Poor-risk NSGCT with unfavorable marker decline after cycle 1 | Germ-cell center referral and consideration of marker-directed intensification[^4] | GETUG-13 selected population[^4]. | Protocol-driven[^4]. |

## Therapy sequencing

| IGCCCG setting or response branch | Treatment order | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Good-risk seminoma or NSGCT | BEP ×3 → response assessment and residual-mass management[^3][^5]. | The cited randomized BEP cycle trial is a good-prognosis population[^3]. |
| Good-risk disease with bleomycin contraindication | EP ×4 → response assessment and residual-mass management[^5]. | Do not substitute EP without explicitly documenting the bleomycin contraindication[^5]. |
| Intermediate-risk seminoma or intermediate-/poor-risk NSGCT | BEP ×4 → response assessment and residual-mass management[^1][^5]. | No poor-prognosis seminoma group exists[^2]. |
| Bleomycin contraindication in intermediate-/poor-risk disease | VIP ×4 with primary G-CSF → response assessment[^5]. | Use an expert germ-cell center; this is not simply an extra drug added to BEP[^5]. |
| Poor-risk NSGCT with unfavorable marker decline after cycle 1 | Refer to a germ-cell center for the GETUG-13-style marker-directed intensification discussion[^4]. | The randomized signal applies only to the selected unfavorable-decline population; it does not establish universal first-line intensification[^4]. |

## Surveillance and treatment de-escalation

| Phase | Monitoring cadence | Stop, hold, or de-escalate | Re-escalation / next line |
|---|---|---|---|
| During BEP/EP/VIP[^5] | Markers before each cycle; imaging after cycle 2–3 and at completion; audiometry and pulmonary function when bleomycin used[^5][^6]. | Complete planned cycles (BEP ×3/×4, EP ×4, VIP ×4); hold bleomycin for pulmonary symptoms; dose-reduce cisplatin for renal toxicity per center policy. GETUG-13 intensification only for selected poor-risk unfavorable marker decline[^3][^4][^5]. | Incomplete marker normalization or growing masses → salvage chemotherapy or post-chemo RPLND per germ-cell center[^5]. |
| Post-chemotherapy remission[^5] | Markers every 1–3 months initially, then extend; imaging of residual masses per guideline (PET for seminoma residual >3 cm; RPLND for NSGCT residual)[^5][^6]. | Stop chemotherapy at protocol completion; no maintenance in standard first-line[^5]. | Relapse → salvage BEP/VIP/high-dose or experimental protocols at expert center[^5]. |

- Residual mass management differs for seminoma (PET-directed observation or radiation) vs NSGCT (RPLND for teratoma/viable GCT)[^5].

| [IGCCCG Update Consortium](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8099402/) | Contemporary metastatic NSGCT receiving first-line cisplatin/etoposide-based therapy[^1] | Prognostic cohort[^1] | No randomized comparator[^1] | Five-year PFS/OS prognosis[^1] | Good/intermediate/poor-risk NSGCT 5-year OS: 96%/89%/67%[^1]. | Prognostic cohort, not regimen-comparison evidence[^1]. |

## Biomarkers

| Marker or feature | Role now | Practical boundary |
|---|---|---|
| AFP, hCG, and LDH | IGCCCG classification and response assessment[^1][^2] | Obtain prechemotherapy values; pure seminoma should not produce AFP[^1][^2]. |
| Early marker decline | Risk refinement in poor-risk disease[^4] | Marker-directed intensification was studied in a selected GETUG-13 population[^4]. |
| FDG-PET | Selected seminoma residual-mass assessment[^5] | Do not use for NSGCT residual disease; a single positive seminoma PET does not establish viable cancer[^5]. |

## Toxicity

| Regimen and comparator | Trial-level safety outcomes | Selected named toxicities | Counseling focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dose-dense intensification versus continued BEP, [GETUG-13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39167741/) | Toxic death 1% in each arm[^4]. | Grade 3–4 neurotoxicity 7% vs 1%; grade 1–2 febrile neutropenia 17% vs 18%[^4]. | Urgent contact for fever, infection, new/worsening neuropathy, dyspnea, dehydration, or severe vomiting[^4]. |
| Standard BEP or VIP | No population-matched primary source with a complete named-event table was retrieved[^3][^5]. | Do not assign unverified rates[^3][^5]. | Counsel on cisplatin renal/hearing/neurologic effects, bleomycin pulmonary risk, myelosuppression, infertility, and survivorship risks[^5]. |

### On-treatment monitoring

| SOC/option regimen | Hallmark monitoring toxicity and available rate | Patient action / clinic response |
|---|---|---|
| BEP | A population-matched primary named-event table was not retrieved[^3][^5]. | Check CBC, renal function, hearing, neuropathy, pulmonary symptoms, nausea/vomiting, and fertility plan; fever, dyspnea, new/worsening numbness, reduced urine output, or uncontrolled vomiting needs urgent contact[^5]. |
| EP | A population-matched primary named-event table was not retrieved[^5]. | Check CBC, renal function, hearing, neuropathy, nausea/vomiting, and fertility plan; fever, bleeding, new/worsening numbness, reduced urine output, or uncontrolled vomiting needs urgent contact[^5]. |
| VIP with primary G-CSF | A population-matched primary named-event table was not retrieved[^5]. | Check CBC, renal function, neurologic symptoms, hemorrhagic-cystitis symptoms, nausea/vomiting, and infection risk; fever, hematuria, confusion, reduced urine output, or uncontrolled vomiting needs urgent contact[^5]. |
| GETUG-13 dose-dense intensification | Grade 3–4 neurotoxicity 7% vs 1%; grade 1–2 febrile neutropenia 17% vs 18%; toxic death 1% in each arm[^4]. | Intensify CBC, neurologic, renal, hearing, pulmonary, and infection monitoring; fever, dyspnea, new/worsening neuropathy, bleeding, or severe vomiting needs urgent assessment[^4]. |
