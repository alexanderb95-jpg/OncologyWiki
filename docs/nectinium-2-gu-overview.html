NECTINIUM-2 (Aktis AKY-1189)

First-in-human Phase 1b theranostic study of a Nectin-4–targeted miniprotein delivering actinium-225, with copper-64 PET for patient selection. For GU oncologists: where it sits relative to EV, what data exist, and what is still unknown.

Design

  • Part 1 (dose escalation): ascending doses of [²²⁵Ac]Ac-AKY-1189 to define MTD/MAD and RP2D; primary endpoints DLTs and AEs. Congress materials describe ~30 patients with locally advanced/metastatic solid tumors including mUC.
  • Part 2 (dose expansion): treat at RP2D in patients identified as Nectin-4 positive by [⁶⁴Cu]Cu-AKY-1189; primary endpoint ORR by tumor type (RECIST 1.1). Planned cohorts include mUC, TNBC, and a solid-tumor basket.

Key eligibility (public materials)

  • Age ≥18; measurable disease (RECIST 1.1); ECOG 0–1; progression on prior metastatic therapy.
  • Congress / TPS language: tumor uptake on [⁶⁴Cu]Cu-AKY-1189 PET/CT confirms eligibility.
  • Prior enfortumab vedotin allowed; other prior Nectin-4–targeted therapy excluded; prior therapeutic radiopharmaceutical excluded.
  • Note: the ClinicalTrials.gov bullet eligibility list is thinner than the ASCO GU / JCO TPS language on PET uptake—use congress text when counseling trial fit.

2. How the theranostic approach works

Same Nectin-4 binder (AKY-1189); isotope chooses the job.

Step 1 · Image Inject [⁶⁴Cu]Cu-AKY-1189 → PET/CT. Ask: does tumor take up the ligand?
Step 2 · Select Public gate = tumor uptake (“Nectin-4 positive by Cu-64 PET”). No published SUVmax / TBR cutoff.
Step 3 · Treat Inject [²²⁵Ac]Ac-AKY-1189. α-particles (high energy, short path length) for localized tumor kill.
This is not EV-based imaging and not an IHC H-score gate in public NECTINIUM-2 materials. Tissue Nectin-4 IHC is background rationale only. Selection is a same-binder PET phenotype.

3. Supporting evidence

Why Nectin-4 is a credible UC target

  • Nectin-4 is highly expressed in urothelial carcinoma and is clinically validated by enfortumab vedotin (MMAE ADC).
  • EV + pembrolizumab is 1L SOC in la/mUC (EV-302); EV monotherapy after platinum + PD-(L)1 (EV-301). That validates the target, not the radioligand modality.

How expression was measured in precursor imaging (not the Cu-64 charter)

  • Metric: lesion SUVmax on multiple ROIs (primary ± mets), ~1–3 h after [⁶⁸Ga]Ga-AKY-1189 (~185 MBq).
  • Binary call in that deck: “uptake positive” vs “Nectin-4 negative” (15/15 evaluable positive).
  • Example SUVmax with [⁶⁸Ga]Ga-AKY-1189 (1 h / 3 h): bone 27.7 / 27.8; lung 10.3 / 16.2; liver 38.5 / 49.3.
  • NECTINIUM-2 switches the imaging isotope to Cu-64; the enrollment SUV rule is not public.

4. Preliminary results (what exists vs what does not)

Do not counsel response rates. No published clinical efficacy for [²²⁵Ac]Ac-AKY-1189 (ORR, DoR, PFS, OS) was found as of 2026-09-22.

Available (imaging / safety / dosimetry — pre–NECTINIUM-2 or concurrent imaging program)

  • [⁶⁸Ga]Ga-AKY-1189 imaging cohort n=20 (bladder primary n=9); 15 evaluable → all uptake-positive.
  • [¹⁷⁷Lu]Lu-AKY-1189 dosimetry subset; reported no AEs attributable to Ga-68 or Lu-177 conjugates in that presentation; one patient received a therapeutic Lu-177 administered activity for therapy/dosimetry.
  • Kidney median absorbed dose ~0.30 Gy/GBq and bone marrow ~0.01 Gy/GBq with Lu-177 (authors compared to ~1 and ~0.1 Gy/GBq benchmarks).
  • Physiologic uptake in salivary/lacrimal glands and GI with washout by 48 h on SPECT in presented cases; kidney flagged as main normal-tissue concern.

NECTINIUM-2 itself (as of ASCO GU 2026 TPS)

  • Actively enrolling in the US; dose escalation ongoing.
  • No dose levels, DLTs, RP2D, or antitumor activity reported in the public TPS poster.

5. What a GU oncologist should know

  1. Modality: α-emitting Nectin-4 radioligand (miniprotein), not an ADC and not EV. Cross-resistance with EV is unknown.
  2. Selection: Companion [⁶⁴Cu]Cu-AKY-1189 PET uptake — not BRCA1, not published IHC H-score, not FGFR/HER2.
  3. Prior EV: Explicitly allowed. Other Nectin-4 drugs (non-EV) and prior therapeutic RPT are exclusions.
  4. Clinic niche today: Trial option after progression on prior metastatic therapy — most relevant post–EV±pembro or later-line mUC when RPT logistics and PET selection are available at a site.
  5. Counseling honesty: Target and imaging look promising; therapeutic Ac-225 benefit and toxicity profile in humans are still being defined in Phase 1b.
  6. Practical logistics: Needs nuclear medicine capable of Cu-64 PET + Ac-225 handling; kidney dosimetry will matter; cycles up to 6 in escalation.
  7. Do not conflate with platinum DDR biomarkers: BRCA1 mRNA/IHC and ERCC2/ATM–RB1–FANCC data apply to cisplatin sensitivity in UC, not to AKY-1189 selection.
Positioning relative to SOC: EV+pembro remains 1L for most la/mUC. NECTINIUM-2 is investigational sequencing after (or instead of, only on trial) standard systemic options—not a replacement pathway.

6. Unanswered questions

Open These are gaps in the public evidence, not protocol criticisms.
  • What is the operational Cu-64 PET cutoff (SUVmax, TBR, visual score, central vs local read)?
  • Does prior EV select for lower surface Nectin-4 and blunt α-RLT activity, or does PET still enrich responders?
  • Will kidney absorbed dose, not marrow or salivary toxicity, set the RP2D?
  • ORR / DoR / PFS in post-EV mUC at RP2D — and how they compare with other post-EV options (FGFR, HER2 ADCs, chemo, trials)?
  • Is there any additive predictive value from tissue Nectin-4 IHC, molecular subtype, or DDR genotype beyond PET uptake?
  • Intralesional heterogeneity: FDG-avid but Cu-64–cold lesions — how often, and clinical meaning?
  • Brain metastases: imaging uptake was shown; therapeutic CNS activity of Ac-225–AKY-1189 is unproven.
  • Combination path (e.g., with PD-1) — not part of NECTINIUM-2 as publicly described.
