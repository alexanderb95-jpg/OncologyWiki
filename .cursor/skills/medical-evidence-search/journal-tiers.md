# Medical Journal Impact Factor Tiers

## Tier 1: Highest Impact (Impact Factor > 50)
- New England Journal of Medicine (NEJM)
- The Lancet
- Nature Medicine
- JAMA (Journal of the American Medical Association)
- BMJ (British Medical Journal)

## Tier 2: Very High Impact (Impact Factor 20-50)
- Nature Reviews (various specialties)
- Cell
- Science Translational Medicine
- JAMA Oncology
- JAMA Surgery
- The Lancet Oncology
- The Lancet Infectious Diseases
- Annals of Internal Medicine

## Tier 3: High Impact (Impact Factor 10-20)
- Journal of Clinical Oncology (JCO)
- Blood
- Cancer Cell
- European Urology
- Journal of the American College of Cardiology
- Circulation
- Gastroenterology
- Hepatology

## Tier 4: Specialty High Impact (Impact Factor 5-10)
- European Urology Oncology
- Journal of Urology
- Urologic Oncology
- Clinical Cancer Research
- Cancer Research
- Journal of the National Cancer Institute

## Specialty Journals (Variable Impact)
- Annals of Oncology
- Journal of Clinical Medicine
- Cancers
- Frontiers in Oncology
- And other domain-specific journals

## Priority Rules

1. **Always prefer Tier 1-2** for general medical questions
2. **Use Tier 3-4** for specialty-specific questions
3. **Recent Tier 3-4** may be preferred over older Tier 1-2 for rapidly evolving fields
4. **Systematic reviews/meta-analyses** from any tier often preferred over single studies
5. **Clinical guidelines** (NCCN, professional societies) take precedence when available
