Who this applies to

KEYNOTE-564 eligibility and decision boundary

Risk groupExact trial definitionAdjuvant discussion
Intermediate-high riskClear-cell RCC: pT2 grade 4 or sarcomatoid, N0 M0; or pT3 any grade, N0 M01Discuss pembrolizumab and risk-adapted surveillance114.
High riskClear-cell RCC: pT4 any grade, N0 M0; or any pT, any grade, N+ M01Discuss pembrolizumab and risk-adapted surveillance114.
M1 NEDPrimary tumor plus solid, isolated, soft-tissue, nonbone/nonbrain metastasis completely resected with negative margins synchronously or within 1 year after nephrectomy; NED at baseline1Discuss in a multidisciplinary setting; the subgroup is small and should not be treated as equivalent to M0 cohorts14.
Lower-risk clear-cell RCCDoes not meet a trial risk definition1Use risk-adapted surveillance; do not extend the trial result to lower-risk disease114.
Non-clear-cell RCCOutside the KEYNOTE-564 population1Do not extrapolate the pembrolizumab result; discuss surveillance or a clinical trial114.
  • KEYNOTE-564 required nephrectomy and, when relevant, metastasectomy within 12 weeks before randomization1. This is trial eligibility, not a universal treatment-start mandate14.
  • Confirm histology, pT/pN stage, grade, sarcomatoid features, margin status, surgery date, NED status, recovery, renal function, autoimmune history, and baseline endocrinopathy before deciding114.

EAU timing after restaging—not trial eligibility

The EAU RCC guideline strongly recommends offering adjuvant pembrolizumab after restaging, preferably 12–16 weeks after nephrectomy, to patients with KEYNOTE-564-defined risk14. It also directs shared discussion of the discordant adjuvant-ICI trials, overtreatment, and immune-related adverse effects14.

Patient counseling and action plan

  • For pembrolizumab, ask patients to contact the oncology team for new thyroid symptoms, including unexplained fatigue, weight or temperature intolerance, or palpitations218. Immune toxicity can begin during or after treatment and may require a hold, corticosteroids, or hormone replacement; endocrine effects can be lasting218.
  • For pembrolizumab plus belzutifan, monitor CBC, ALT, and oxygen saturation19. Ask patients to contact the oncology team for new fatigue, lightheadedness, or shortness of breath; marked breathing difficulty needs urgent assessment19.
  • For belzutifan, review the label-specific embryo-fetal warning and pregnancy-prevention counseling19. This clinically critical warning is retained despite not being a numeric trial-toxicity row19.

  • Counseling: Reviewed pathology-based recurrence risk after nephrectomy with the KEYNOTE-564 eligibility calculator: Not selected. This calculator classifies the published trial-risk category; it does not replace pathology review or patient-specific treatment assessment.
  • Counseling: Reviewed adjuvant pembrolizumab and risk-adapted surveillance. In KEYNOTE-564, at 5 years estimated disease-free survival was about 61 in 100 vs 52 in 100 with pembrolizumab versus placebo, and overall survival about 88 in 100 vs 82 in 100 (published 60.9% vs 52.2% DFS; 87.7% vs 82.3% OS). Trial compared pembrolizumab to placebo after surgery, not to surveillance alone. Results apply to the trial population and do not establish benefit in lower-risk or non-clear-cell RCC. US label also permits pembrolizumab plus belzutifan with immature OS versus pembrolizumab alone.
  • Counseling: Reviewed surveillance and treatment de-escalation: risk-adapted imaging every 3–6 months for 3 years then annually if observation selected; if adjuvant pembrolizumab (± belzutifan), complete up to the labeled year unless toxicity or progression, then continue imaging surveillance. Recurrence → metastatic RCC pathway.
