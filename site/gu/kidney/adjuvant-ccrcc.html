RCC → Adjuvant clear-cell RCC

Last reviewed: 2026-09-21 · Next review: 2026-12-20

current — pembrolizumab has randomized DFS and OS benefit; in the US, pembrolizumab plus belzutifan is FDA-labeled with immature OS

Who this applies to

KEYNOTE-564 eligibility and decision boundary

Risk groupExact trial definitionAdjuvant discussion
Intermediate-high riskClear-cell RCC: pT2 grade 4 or sarcomatoid, N0 M0; or pT3 any grade, N0 M0Discuss pembrolizumab and risk-adapted surveillance.
High riskClear-cell RCC: pT4 any grade, N0 M0; or any pT, any grade, N+ M0Discuss pembrolizumab and risk-adapted surveillance.
M1 NEDPrimary tumor plus solid, isolated, soft-tissue, nonbone/nonbrain metastasis completely resected with negative margins synchronously or within 1 year after nephrectomy; NED at baselineDiscuss in a multidisciplinary setting; the subgroup is small and should not be treated as equivalent to M0 cohorts.
Lower-risk clear-cell RCCDoes not meet a trial risk definitionUse risk-adapted surveillance; do not extend the trial result to lower-risk disease.
Non-clear-cell RCCOutside the KEYNOTE-564 populationDo not extrapolate the pembrolizumab result; discuss surveillance or a clinical trial.
  • KEYNOTE-564 required nephrectomy and, when relevant, metastasectomy within 12 weeks before randomization. This is trial eligibility, not a universal treatment-start mandate.
  • Confirm histology, pT/pN stage, grade, sarcomatoid features, margin status, surgery date, NED status, recovery, renal function, autoimmune history, and baseline endocrinopathy before deciding.

EAU timing after restaging—not trial eligibility

The EAU RCC guideline strongly recommends offering adjuvant pembrolizumab after restaging, preferably 12–16 weeks after nephrectomy, to patients with KEYNOTE-564-defined risk. It also directs shared discussion of the discordant adjuvant-ICI trials, overtreatment, and immune-related adverse effects.

Patient counseling and action plan

  • Thyroid dysfunction was the most common immune toxicity in KEYNOTE-564: about 21 in 100 patients developed hypothyroidism and 13 in 100 hyperthyroidism. Grade 3 adrenal insufficiency, diabetic ketoacidosis, and type 1 diabetes each occurred in about 1 in 100. Review new fatigue, temperature intolerance, palpitations, dizziness, severe headache, thirst, confusion, or nausea promptly; hypothyroidism can require long-term replacement.
  • In KEYNOTE-564, treatment-related grade 3 diarrhea occurred in 2 in 100 and grade 3 colitis in 1 in 100; grade 3 ALT elevation occurred in 2 in 100 and AST elevation in 1 in 100. Contact oncology the same day for diarrhea, severe abdominal pain, fever, blood/mucus in stool, jaundice, dark urine, severe nausea/vomiting, or unusual bruising.
  • In KEYNOTE-564, pneumonitis occurred in 1.6%; grade 3/4 pneumonitis, grade 3 nephritis, and grade 3 Stevens–Johnson syndrome were each <1%. New cough, chest pain, dyspnea, markedly reduced urine, or new swelling needs prompt contact; marked breathing difficulty or a widespread painful, blistering, peeling, or mucosal rash needs urgent assessment.
  • Grade 3/4 cardiovascular, neurologic, hematologic, and other treatment-related events in KEYNOTE-564 were generally <1%, except grade 3 hypertension at 1%. Seek urgent assessment for chest pain, fainting, palpitations, focal neurologic symptoms, severe weakness, abnormal bleeding, or severe infection symptoms.
  • With pembrolizumab plus belzutifan, about 84 in 100 developed anemia and 12 in 100 had grade ≥3 anemia; hypoxia occurred in about 7 in 100 and was grade ≥3 in about 5 in 100. Call promptly for worsening fatigue, dizziness, palpitations, chest discomfort, dyspnea, or a low home pulse-oximeter reading. Check hemoglobin and oxygen saturation before treatment and periodically; severe events may require transfusion, oxygen, and belzutifan hold, dose reduction, or discontinuation.
  • For the combination, grade ≥3 ALT elevation was 6.4%, and the same-trial label table reports grade 3–4 fatigue 3.1% and diarrhea 3.0%. Continue CBC/liver testing and report jaundice, dark urine, persistent diarrhea, severe nausea, disabling dizziness/fatigue, fever, or infection symptoms promptly.
  • Give patients the clinic's day-and-after-hours number. Ask them to call for new symptoms rather than waiting for the next infusion; immune toxicities can begin during therapy or after pembrolizumab has stopped and may require treatment interruption, corticosteroids, hormone replacement, or specialist care.
  • Before and during pembrolizumab, review autoimmune history, immunosuppression, and baseline endocrine disease; check CBC, creatinine/liver tests, and thyroid function per the KEYTRUDA label and institutional protocol. Add hemoglobin and pulse-oximetry monitoring for belzutifan.
  • For belzutifan, review the boxed embryo-fetal warning and label-specific pregnancy-prevention counseling before treatment.
